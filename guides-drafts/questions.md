# Questions for the NXTREV team

Answer in any format: voice note, email, bullet points. Short answers are fine. Skip anything you can't share, and say "don't publish" next to anything that is for context only.

## For every guide
1. Whose name should appear as author, and who reviews the technical content?
2. Can we describe past work in general terms ("a Philippine OSAT", "a global IDM's backend line")? Anything that must never be mentioned?

## Guide 1: Choosing a test handler for your package
Suggested person: applications or field engineer.
1. What are the three questions you ask a customer first when they say "we need a handler"?
2. Which package families come up most in Philippine plants right now?
3. What is the most common mistake you see when a plant picks a handler (wrong format, wrong site count, contact problems)?
4. Typical changeover between packages on a turret handler with a conversion kit: what drives the time?
5. When does strip test beat a turret handler for the same device, and when doesn't it?
6. For power discretes: why does Kelvin contact isolation matter in practice, with one example?
7. Anything a buyer should prepare before asking for a handler quote (sample devices, drawings, UPH target)?

## Guide 2: STS8600, STS8300 or STS8200 CROSS?
Suggested person: ATE applications engineer.
1. Typical devices you have seen evaluated on each platform in the Philippines (device type only, no customer).
2. How does an AccoTEST evaluation work: what does the customer provide, how long, what decides pass or fail?
3. What does "test program conversion" involve when a plant moves a device from another tester to AccoTEST? Typical effort?
4. How many parallel sites do PMIC customers actually run on the STS8300, and what limits it?
5. When does a power device need the AXE-PLUS rather than AXE-PM or AXE-FET?
6. What local support does NXTREV provide after installation (applications, spares, training)?

## Guide 3: Bringing test equipment into a PEZA zone
Suggested person: Elmer or commercial operations.
1. Walk through the steps from purchase order to equipment on the floor. Who does each step: NXTREV, the customer, the forwarder, the broker, PEZA?
2. Which documents are needed, and which ones cause delays most often?
3. Typical total time from shipment to delivery at a PEZA site, and what makes it longer?
4. What should the customer arrange before shipment (permits, site readiness, power, floor space)?
5. Anything different for pre-owned equipment or spare boards?

## Guide 4: From delivery to buy-off
Suggested person: Elmer or the field service lead.
1. List the steps from equipment arrival to production release, in order.
2. What exactly is pre-verified before delivery (docking, load board tolerance, socket contact pressure)? How?
3. What are the acceptance criteria usually agreed: correlation limits, Gage R&R target, yield threshold?
4. The site says qualification cycles "drop from months to weeks". Is there a real example behind that, and can the numbers be shared in general terms?
5. What most often delays buy-off, and how do you prevent it?
6. What does support look like in the first three months after release?

## Guide 5: Keeping an ASL1000 fleet running
Suggested person: board repair engineer.
1. Which ASL1000 boards fail most often, and what are the usual failure causes?
2. Typical repair turnaround, and what affects it?
3. How does a resource module conversion (for example HVS850 into DVI300) work, and when is it worth it?
4. What does the caldut buy-off and the multi-hour burn-in validation involve?
5. How do you source and verify pre-owned mainframes and boards? What does the diagnostics report include?
6. What should a plant keep in stock on site?
