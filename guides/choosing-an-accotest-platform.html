STS8600, STS8300 or STS8200 CROSS? Matching AccoTEST Platforms to Your Device

Three platform families, three kinds of device. How to tell which AccoTEST tester fits yours, and what an evaluation in the Philippines looks like.

By John Eliel C. Baladjay, Business Operations Manager · September 25, 2026 · 4 min read

AccoTEST, made by Beijing Huafeng Test & Control Technology (STAR: 688200), has three platform families. The question I hear most from engineers is simple: which one is mine? The answer depends on the device, not on which tester is newest.

NXTREV is AccoTEST's authorized sales representative in the Philippines, so this is the conversation we have every week. Here is how it usually goes.

STS8600: SoC and high-speed digital

The STS8600 uses a liquid-cooled test head for complex analog and high-speed digital pins, aimed at data centre, automotive and IoT devices. AccoTEST describes it as "One Platform for All" for high-pin-count processors, automotive microcontrollers and high-speed digital devices.

If most of your test time goes into digital patterns and you are running out of pins or speed on your current platform, start here.

Prober and Handler Docking: Direct hard-dock and cable interface kits support standard industry wafer probers (Tokyo Seimitsu / ACCRETECH UF200 and UF3000, Tokyo Electron / TEL Precio and P12XL) as well as high-speed package handlers (Chroma, Hontech, Multitest). We assist with docking alignment, manipulator counterbalance, and custom load board (DIB) layout.

STS8300: PMIC and mixed-signal

The STS8300 is built for parallel test. Its all-in-one test head runs up to 32 sites for PMICs, smart power stages, integrated passive devices and battery management devices.

The site count you actually run is usually set by the handler and the load board, not the tester. Before you plan around 32 sites, check how many sites your handler can present and whether your load board layout keeps every site within spec.

Docking & Parallel Production Cells: Rigid mechanical docking integrates cleanly with standard pick-and-place and turret test handlers (Chroma, Hontech, Multitest) and wafer probers (ACCRETECH, TEL). For up to 32 parallel sites, rigid docking mechanics maintain planarity and signal integrity across high-pin-count Kelvin contactors.

STS8200 CROSS: power discretes and modules

The STS8200 CROSS is a universal power discrete and module tester for wide-bandgap SiC and GaN, MOSFETs, IGBTs, known good die and automotive power bricks. It comes in three configurations:

  • AXE-PM for power modules
  • AXE-FET for GaN and SiC FETs
  • AXE-PLUS, the universal configuration with the highest power envelope

Test programs are developed in open C++.

A recent example: a customer sent us the test plan for a new power package and asked whether the STS8200 already on their floor could handle it. AccoTEST's applications engineers reviewed it and confirmed the avalanche (UIS), DC and gate resistance tests were all within the machine's rated specs. The hardware answer was yes. The real lead time was the test program, because none existed yet for that device.

Docking for Power & Wafer Sort: Optimized for high-voltage and high-current docking to wafer probers (ACCRETECH UF-series, TEL) for discrete wafer sort (AXE-FET), as well as gravity-feed and strip-test handlers (Multitest, Hontech) for packaged power modules (AXE-PM). Heavy-duty Kelvin docking interfaces eliminate contact resistance and thermal drift during 2000A DC pulses.

How an evaluation works

We have placed an AccoTEST evaluation tester at a Philippine OSAT, handling principal coordination, the free-zone shipment and on-site setup. From that experience, this is what to expect:

  1. You share the device and test plan. Under NDA if needed. The applications team checks every test against the platform's specs and tells you what the configuration needs.
  2. We agree the loan terms. A short loan agreement covers the unit, its condition, the duration and who is responsible for what. Your legal team will likely want to review it, so start early; this step took longer than the shipping.
  3. Permits and delivery. If your plant is inside an economic zone or freeport, the permit to bring the unit in is filed on your side. Our guide to bringing equipment into a PEZA or freeport zone covers what the permit needs.
  4. Setup on the day. An AccoTEST engineer comes with the delivery and sets up the tester, so it can start work the same week.
  5. Program development and correlation. For a new device, the team writes and debugs the program: optimization, spike checks, and correlation against your reference data. A manual socket helps here, because you can start correlating before the handler is docked.

A tip on timing: plan the evaluation around your own events and holidays. We once had to schedule a short pull-out mid-loan for an exhibition and bring the unit back afterwards. It worked, but only because everyone knew the dates weeks ahead.

Moving a device from another tester

Test program conversion means rebuilding your existing test flow on the new platform: same tests, same limits, new code and new hardware interface. The effort depends mostly on how well the original program is documented. A clean test plan with limits and conditions for every test makes it a straightforward job. A program that only exists as code on the old tester takes longer, because someone has to read it back into a test plan first.

Correlation is what decides whether the conversion is done: the same units, tested on both platforms, must agree within the limits you set up front.

Local support after installation

We provide factory-certified applications support for all three platforms, including test program conversion, pin-count feasibility and prober or handler docking integration with standard industry platforms (Tokyo Seimitsu / ACCRETECH and TEL probers; Chroma, Hontech, and Multitest pick-and-place/turret handlers). AccoTEST's own engineers in the region work with us on setup and training, so you have one local contact and a factory team behind it.
